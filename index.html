About

Statistical and machine learning methods that turn large-scale single-cell, spatial and multimodal cancer data into reproducible biological discoveries and clinically actionable insights.

Cancer is a spatially organized ecosystem in which tumor initiation, evolution, metastasis and treatment response emerge from the coordinated behavior of malignant and non-malignant cells. My research moves from statistical rigor to biological discovery to clinical translation: (1) developing principled methods that separate biological signal from unwanted variation, building on the RUV framework (RUV-III, PRPS, RUVprps, fastRUV-III); (2) decoding how cell states, cell–cell interactions, spatial niches and multicellular programs organize and reorganize during metastasis, organ colonization and therapy; and (3) translating these high-resolution discoveries into biomarkers and predictive models that work on routine H&E histopathology and bulk transcriptomics.

Research

Three interconnected aims, built on the STAMP atlas

Aim 1 · Methods

Robust & reproducible cancer genomics

Principled, scalable statistical methods that distinguish biological signal from unwanted technical and confounding variation across studies, platforms and modalities.

Details
  • The RUV framework — RUV-III for NanoString nCounter (Nucleic Acids Res 2019), pseudo-replicates of pseudo-samples (PRPS) for large-scale RNA-seq (Nat Biotechnol 2023), and RUVprps, which identifies unwanted variation without prior knowledge across TCGA, CCLE, GTEx and Treehouse.
  • Atlas-scale single-cell & spatial — RUV-III-NB for single-cell counts and fastRUV-III for single-cell, CITE-seq, ATAC-seq and spatial data, normalizing millions of cells in minutes.
  • Next steps — cell-type- and spatially aware models, joint normalization of multimodal (RNA + ADT) data, and count-aware estimators that scale beyond 10 million cells.
  • Digital pathology — removing staining-center and scanner effects from foundation-model image embeddings to improve model transportability across institutions.
  • Confounding & diagnostics — identifiability theory, sensitivity analyses and RLE-based diagnostics for when biology and unwanted variation are intertwined.
Aim 2 · Discovery

Cellular & spatial organization of cancer ecosystems

Scalable, interpretable methods that resolve cell states, spatial niches and multicellular programs across millions of cells and thousands of patients.

Details
  • Reproducible cell states — reference-free annotation with ClustoCell, evaluated on ~24 million cells, extended with hierarchical models, calibrated uncertainty and detection of novel or transitional states.
  • Multicellular modules — composition-aware count models that bring the RUV principle to cell-state abundances, separating true cellular coordination from study, platform and dissociation artifacts.
  • Ecosystem rewiring — how ecosystems reorganize across normal tissue, primary tumors, metastatic sites and treatment, separating tumor-intrinsic programs from host-organ signal.
  • Tissue-of-sampling confounding — a framework showing that host-tissue signal can masquerade as metastatic tumor biology in bulk transcriptomes.
Aim 3 · Translation

Clinically accessible biomarkers & prediction

Transferring discoveries from single-cell and spatial profiling to routine H&E histopathology and bulk transcriptomics to predict progression and treatment response.

Details
  • Spatial biology from H&E — attention-based multiple-instance learning on pathology foundation-model embeddings to predict spatial ecotypes and cell states from routine slides.
  • Bulk RNA-seq — uncertainty-aware deconvolution and cross-modal transfer learning to estimate atlas-defined cell states, modules and host-tissue contributions.
  • Clinical trials — multimodal immune profiling (spatial, CITE-seq, IMC, methylation, PET) of response to 177Lu-PSMA, pembrolizumab and olaparib in metastatic prostate cancer.
  • Consortia — integration of ~2,000 prostate cancer transcriptomes from eight countries for the Pan Prostate Cancer Group.

Atlas · single-cell, bulk & spatial

STAMP

Single-cell Transcriptomic Atlas of Metastatic and Primary tumors: ~18 million single cells from 5,500 samples, 70,000 bulk transcriptomes and 54 million cells and spots from 2,400 spatial transcriptomic samples, uniformly processed, quality-controlled and normalized.

Details
  • Status — manuscript in final preparation; public release planned through an interactive web resource.
  • Single-cell — computational pipelines for cell-type and cell-state annotation across cancer types and disease sites.
  • Bulk — major sources of unwanted variation identified, molecular subtypes characterized, and cellular composition estimated using the single-cell atlas as a reference.
  • Spatial & H&E — matched spatial profiling and histopathology for studying tissue organization and training image-based predictors.
  • Growing — an automated search and data-ingestion pipeline brings in newly released datasets, with priority on multimodal, emerging single-cell and spatial technologies.
  • Purpose — a resource for studying cancer evolution across primary and metastatic disease, and a demanding test bed for new statistical methods.
  • Developed at DFCI (Beltran lab), 2025–2026. Preliminary results presented at AACR 2026.

R package · bulk RNA-seq

RUVprps

Accurate and scalable normalization and integration of large and complex RNA-seq data.

Details
  • Status — completed; preprint under revision at Nucleic Acids Research.
  • Method — identifies sources of unwanted variation directly from the data and constructs pseudo-replicates of pseudo-samples (PRPS) without prior knowledge of biological or unwanted variation.
  • Validated on TCGA, CCLE, GTEx and Treehouse.
  • Scope — more than 100 functions for assessing, removing and diagnosing unwanted variation.
  • Developed at WEHI and DFCI, 2024–2026.

R package · single-cell & spatial

fastRUV-III

Fast normalization and integration of atlas-scale single-cell, multimodal and spatial data.

Details
  • Status — completed; manuscript in final preparation.
  • Data — single-cell RNA-seq, CITE-seq (ADT), ATAC-seq, and imaging- and sequencing-based spatial assays.
  • Speed — normalizes 5 million cells across 8,000 genes in under five minutes on a single CPU core.
  • Benchmarks — performs strongly against scVI, Harmony and BBKNN.
  • Related — RUV-III-NB for single-cell counts (Nucleic Acids Res 2022).
  • Developed at WEHI and DFCI, 2024–2026.
GitHub
Method · single-cell

ClustoCell

Reference-free identification of cell identities, cell states and their markers from single-cell transcriptomes.

Details
  • Status — preprint, manuscript under review.
  • Approach — stratifies expression within each cell into high- and intermediate-expression tiers to build similarity graphs driven by intrinsic expression programs.
  • Scale — evaluated across ~24 million cells spanning multiple tissues, diseases and platforms.
  • Role — senior author; development supervised at PMCC and DFCI.
Preprint
R package · metastasis

RUVprps_Met

Removal of host-tissue background signal from metastatic bulk RNA-seq.

Details
  • Status — completed; manuscript in final preparation.
  • Problem — the tissue a metastasis is sampled from (e.g. bone) can contribute strong host-tissue programs that are mistaken for tumour biology.
  • Approach — uses STAMP single-cell and spatial data to estimate host-tissue, immune and stromal contributions, and removes them within the RUVprps framework.
  • Developed at DFCI, 2026.
